A working grammar for Kashmiri.

A practical scheme for reading, writing, checking and teaching modern Kashmiri in the Perso-Arabic script. It brings the writing system, sound patterns, word structure, sentence structure and proofreading decisions into one place.

How to use this guide. This is a careful working reference, not a claim that every Kashmiri speaker uses one identical form. Kashmiri has regional, social and script traditions. The examples and orthography rules are tied to the sources listed at the end; uncertain grammar should be checked with a fluent speaker before publication.

1. Scope and conventions

Kashmiri, or کٲشُر, is an Indo-Aryan language of the Dardic group. This guide is for modern Kashmiri written in the Perso-Arabic Nastaliq tradition. Kashmiri is also written in Devanagari; the language is the same, but the character, direction and spelling rules differ.

Use the language tag ks for Kashmiri, ks-Arab for Perso-Arabic Kashmiri and ks-Deva for Devanagari Kashmiri. Use right-to-left direction for the Arabic script. Keep Kashmiri text, English glosses and transliteration in separate fields so a reader can tell what is original and what is an aid.

The guide uses these labels: word means a dictionary headword; form means an inflection or spelling variant; gloss means a short English meaning; transliteration means a pronunciation-oriented Latin rendering; proofreading means checking an already written sentence, not inventing a translation.

2. Script and spelling

Direction, shape and Unicode

Kashmiri Arabic text runs right to left and is normally displayed in Nastaliq. Letters join in context, so the visible shape is not a separate character. Store ordinary Unicode letters and combining marks, never screenshots, presentation forms or manually drawn glyphs. Normalize text to NFC before comparison. Do not insert tatweel merely to make a word look longer.

The modern orthography is unusually vowel-explicit for an Arabic-derived script. Vowel sounds are represented with letters and combining marks, and vowel marks should not be removed from a carefully edited Kashmiri text. A word that looks understandable after the marks are deleted may still become ambiguous or non-standard.

Core inventory

The orthography reference describes 21 basic consonant letters, six aspirated consonant digraphs and additional letters used for Persian, Arabic and Urdu loans. Palatalisation has a dedicated Kashmiri sign. Word-initial vowels use a carrier; medial and final vowels can use different Unicode sequences. Follow the character tables in the orthography reference when choosing a code point.

3. Sounds and transliteration

Kashmiri contrasts plain and aspirated stops, dental and retroflex consonants, several fricatives, palatalised consonants and a rich vowel system with central and nasalised qualities. The exact phonetic value of a letter depends on its position and its neighbours. Transliteration is therefore a reading aid, not a reversible replacement for Kashmiri spelling.

4. How words are built

Kashmiri word forms carry information about number, gender, case, person, tense, aspect and agreement. A dictionary headword is only one point in a paradigm. When writing a sentence, check the inflected form rather than forcing the bare headword into every position.

Compounds and borrowed words

Compounds, reduplication, inherited Indo-Aryan vocabulary and Persian/Arabic/Urdu loans all occur in modern Kashmiri. A borrowed consonant or spelling should not be “purified” into a native sound if the source intentionally preserves the loan. Mark a loan or a proper name as such in editorial metadata.

Clitics and short forms

Short pronoun, copula and particle forms can attach phonologically to a neighbouring word. In a searchable dictionary, store both the surface phrase and the component analysis when known. In running text, follow the source’s spacing and do not split a clitic simply because English would use a separate word.

Inflection

Record a form with its grammatical features: singular, plural, masculine, feminine, direct, oblique, ergative, past, imperfective or perfective. If a form is not supported by a source, label it as a hypothesis instead of presenting it as a rule.

5. Nouns, pronouns and cases

Nouns and adjectives

Nouns have grammatical gender and number. Adjectives and participles may agree with the noun, the subject or the absolutive argument, depending on construction. Agreement is a sentence-level check: do not choose an adjective ending from the English noun alone.

Case is commonly expressed through oblique forms, particles and postpositions. Postpositions follow their noun phrase, as in many Indo-Aryan languages. A noun before a postposition may therefore look different from the same noun used as a bare subject or object.

Pronouns

Pronoun forms have direct, oblique and case-marked uses. A polite or plural second-person form should not be replaced automatically with a singular form. In proofreading, identify the intended person and number first, then check the pronoun, its case marker and the verb or copula agreement together.

Case checklist

6. Sentences and word order

The safe default for a simple Kashmiri clause is subject–object–verb, with modifiers before the noun and postpositions after the noun phrase. Information structure can move a topic or focus to the front, and finite verbs can show a verb-second pattern in independent clauses. A sentence may therefore be grammatical even when it is not a rigid SOV string.

Build a sentence in layers: (1) choose the predicate, (2) identify its arguments, (3) assign case, (4) select tense/aspect and agreement, (5) add negation or question marking, and (6) check the orthography. This order prevents an English word order from dictating Kashmiri endings.

Phrase structure

  • Noun phrase: determiner/quantifier + adjective or modifier + noun + case/postposition.
  • Verb phrase: lexical verb or participle + auxiliary/copula and agreement material.
  • Subordination: the subordinate clause normally comes before the main clause; use the attested linker or participle.
  • Coordination: preserve the conjunction and repeat agreement only where the source grammar requires it.

Keep names, quotations, numbers and web addresses as protected spans during proofreading. They should not be sent through a Kashmiri spell-change rule.

7. Verbs, tense, aspect and agreement

Verb information

Every finite verb should be analysed for lexical root, aspect, tense or mood, person, number and any gender agreement. Kashmiri uses auxiliaries and copular material extensively. The infinitive and the stem are not interchangeable, and a participle is not automatically a finite verb.

For a practical analysis, annotate a form like this: ROOT – TAM – AGR – POLARITY. For example, a learner’s note might say “read – perfective – first-person – affirmative”; the Kashmiri surface form must still be checked against a reliable paradigm.

Split alignment

Descriptions of Kashmiri report a split between present/intransitive patterns and many past transitive patterns. In a past transitive clause, the agent can receive ergative marking and agreement may be controlled by the absolutive argument or object. This is why translating “I read a book” by selecting a generic first-person verb ending can produce an agreement error. Check the tense, transitivity and object together.

Copulas and auxiliaries

Copular and auxiliary forms vary with person, number, gender and construction. Do not copy an English “is/are/was” into every clause. When a sentence contains a copula, check the subject or absolutive noun phrase and the polarity before changing the form.

8. Negation and questions

Negation

Negation is grammatical, not merely punctuation. A negative particle or auxiliary can change the position and form of the copula or verb. Identify the construction first—nominal, copular, existential, verbal or imperative—then use the negative form recorded for that construction. Do not mechanically place English not before the Kashmiri verb.

Questions

Yes/no questions use Kashmiri syntax and intonation, with the question mark ؟ in writing where appropriate. Question words normally occupy the same argument position as the answer they request; English subject–auxiliary inversion should not be copied. A wh-question can still contain the same case and agreement markings as a statement.

Imperatives and politeness

Imperative forms depend on person, number and politeness. A command to one familiar person is not interchangeable with a respectful or plural request. Keep the addressee in mind when proofreading and preserve honorific material supplied by the writer.

9. The proofreading scheme

Use this order. It catches the cheap errors first and leaves dialect-sensitive decisions for a fluent reviewer.

  1. Protect spans. Mark names, quotations, URLs, email addresses, numbers and code before editing.
  2. Normalize encoding. Apply NFC, remove accidental zero-width characters and reject presentation-form glyphs.
  3. Check script. Confirm the sentence is intentionally Perso-Arabic Kashmiri; flag accidental Urdu, Arabic or Persian substitutions.
  4. Check graphemes. Verify Kashmiri kaf, yeh, heh, palatalisation, aspiration, nasalisation, vowel marks and initial-vowel carriers.
  5. Check word boundaries. Confirm clitics, compounds, postpositions and punctuation against an attested source.
  6. Check the predicate. Identify transitivity and TAM before checking agreement.
  7. Check noun phrases. Verify gender, number, direct/oblique form and case marking.
  8. Check polarity and questions. Ensure the negative or interrogative construction is the intended one.
  9. Check meaning. Compare the sentence with its context; a dictionary gloss cannot decide a homograph or dialect form.
  10. Generate transliteration last. Transliteration should reflect the final Kashmiri spelling, never replace it.
  11. Record uncertainty. If two forms are plausible, keep both with a note and ask a fluent speaker instead of silently selecting one.

Proofreading rule: a normalizer may fix ك to ک or remove a zero-width space, but it must not invent a word ending, case marker or translation. Automatic correction and grammatical proofreading are separate passes.

For a new sentence, write the Kashmiri line, add the English gloss separately, then add transliteration. Do not use the English gloss as a back-translation test unless a fluent speaker has checked the intended meaning.

11. Sources, review and limits

The script and Unicode rules on this page follow the bundled Kashmiri Arabic orthography notes v32. Examples and lexical evidence come from the attributed dictionary records and the page-linked OCR collections described in SOURCES.md. OCR vocabulary is useful for discovery and spelling comparison, but an OCR occurrence is not by itself an English definition or a grammatical paradigm.

The site can normalize common character variants, search transliterations and run a browser translation model. It cannot infer a missing dialect rule, guarantee a native-speaker interpretation or prove that a rare OCR word is correct. A living language needs continuing review by Kashmiri speakers, teachers, writers and linguists. Every proposed correction should retain its original text, source, date and reviewer note.

For contribution review, submit: the Kashmiri sentence; a transliteration; an English gloss; dialect/region; grammatical analysis; source or speaker; and the exact change being proposed.

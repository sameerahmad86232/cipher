## Lesson 2 — verified as supplied
7. She said to me. — تٔمۍ ووٚن مےٚ
8. They said to me. — تمو ووٚن میہ ۔
9. He gave food to that person. — Temov deuot aiemis nafres kean khean
10. They did not give food to that person. — Temov deuot ne aiemis nafres kean khean

**Local patterns only:** `ووٚن` occurs in both reporting sentences. `تٔمۍ` and `تمو`, and `مےٚ` and `میہ`, contrast in the supplied examples; full morphological rules are not yet established. `ne` appears after `deuot` in the last example and was not present in the preceding example; this does not prove a universal negation rule because the English subjects differ.

**Open:** `Temov` was also used for the Lesson 1 *they gave* example, but Lesson 2 Example 9 was offered for *he gave*. Do not infer it is unambiguously singular or plural. Preserve differences between `deout` and `deuot`, and `temis` and `aiemis`. Do not normalize without the owner's confirmation.

### Supported local observations

- Both Lesson 5 examples use `Suoa` with the English subject **he**. Lesson 4 also has `Suoa` for a **she** affirmative sentence. This usage does **not** establish a universal masculine or feminine form.
- `ne` appears in the negative but not the affirmative Lesson 5 sentence, consistent with earlier negative examples. Only this specific contrast is confirmed, not a universal future-tense negation rule.
- The forms `waenii` versus `waeni`, and `kaenh nateei keanh` versus `keaheene`, differ beyond `ne`. Do not silently correct or normalize them or assign individual morpheme meanings without evidence.
- Compare Lesson 4: She (affirmative) `Suoa waeniie mea keanh naete keanh`; She (negative) `Suo waeniie ne mea keheeene`. No reliable singular masculine/feminine future conjugation paradigm has yet been established.

## Lesson 2 — verified as supplied
7. She said to me. — تٔمۍ ووٚن مےٚ
8. They said to me. — تمو ووٚن میہ ۔
9. He gave food to that person. — Temov deuot aiemis nafres kean khean
10. They did not give food to that person. — Temov deuot ne aiemis nafres kean khean

**Local patterns only:** `ووٚن` occurs in both reporting sentences. `تٔمۍ` and `تمو`, and `مےٚ` and `میہ`, contrast in the supplied examples; full morphological rules are not yet established. `ne` appears after `deuot` in the last example and was not present in the preceding example; this does not prove a universal negation rule because the English subjects differ.

**Open:** `Temov` was also used for the Lesson 1 *they gave* example, but Lesson 2 Example 9 was offered for *he gave*. Do not infer it is unambiguously singular or plural. Preserve differences between `deout` and `deuot`, and `temis` and `aiemis`. Do not normalize without the owner's confirmation.

### Attested observations — not universal rules

- In these paired contexts, the English male subject corresponds to `Su che dewaan ...` and the English female subject corresponds to `Sua chae dewaaan ...`. The owner had earlier provided standalone `سُہ` for *he* and `سۅ` for *she*; no formal mapping from the new Romanizations to those script forms is certified.
- `humis` appears in both recipient phrases, and the owner explicitly glosses `humis` as *that* in the second.
- The second original glosses `dewaaan` as *giving* and `khean` as *food*. Both phrases contain `khean`.
- `che` versus `chae`, `dewaan` versus `dewaaan`, and `naferaaas` versus `naferass` are observed differences, **not yet confirmed** as grammatical agreement or case rules.
- These two sentences are translations of *is giving*, but they do not independently establish a complete present-tense system or the rules for habitual *gives*.

## Reuse and approval policy
- Keep exact Romanized or Kashmiri-script spellings, punctuation and alternatives in the source records.
- Distinguish *native-speaker example* from *inferred grammatical rule*.
- Cross-check each Hadith against Arabic and a careful English gloss, including subject, tense, negation, conditional clauses and religious register.
- Do not treat AI-drafted text as gold grammar evidence and do not mark a full Hadith approved without explicit owner approval.
- Read also `HADITH-TRANSLATION-METHOD.md`, `PARALLEL-GRAMMAR-FINDINGS.md`, `user-approved-grammar-pairs.json` and the separately recorded owner corrections.

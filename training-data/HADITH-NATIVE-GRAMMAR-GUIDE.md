Version 1.4 — owner-supplied evidence, October 29, 2026.

This file is a working grammatical reference, not a certified universal grammar. Preserve the Kashmiri exactly as supplied. The 26 formerly owner-approved Hadith and owner-reviewed vocabulary remain independent evidence, and grammar examples do not approve a full Hadith.

## Lesson 1 — verified as supplied
1. He said to me. — Team woun mea
2. They gave food to that person. — Temov deout temis nafras kheain
3. When he speaks, he tells a lie. — Yeli te su wanan chu, su chu apuz wanan
4. If someone makes a promise, they should keep it. — اگر توہہِ کانہہ وعدہ کران چھِو، توہہِ پزِ اتھ پیٹھ قائم روزُن۔
5. Whoever does a good deed will receive its reward. — یُس کانٛہہ تہِ اصل کٲم کران چھُ تٔمِس میلہِ امُک انعام۔
6. If it had not caused hardship for my community, I would have gone with them. — اگر نہٕ امہِ سۭتۍ میٲنِ برادری  (یا امت) خٲطرٕ مسلہٕ پٲدٕ گژھہِ نہٕ، بہٕ آسہٕ ہا تِمن سۭتۍ گٔومُت۔

**Correction to Example 2:** The owner explicitly removed a duplicated temis. Use exactly one temis.
**Open:** Example 4 addresses the second person in Kashmiri unlike the generic third-person English prompt; Example 6's negation scope and alternatives need checking.

## Lesson 2 — verified as supplied
7. She said to me. — تٔمۍ ووٚن مےٚ
8. They said to me. — تمو ووٚن میہ ۔
9. He gave food to that person. — Temov deuot aiemis nafres kean khean
10. They did not give food to that person. — Temov deuot ne aiemis nafres kean khean

**Local patterns only:** `ووٚن` occurs in both reporting sentences. `تٔمۍ` and `تمو`, and `مےٚ` and `میہ`, contrast in the supplied examples; full morphological rules are not yet established. `ne` appears after `deuot` in the last example and was not present in the preceding example; this does not prove a universal negation rule because the English subjects differ.

**Open:** `Temov` was also used for the Lesson 1 *they gave* example, but Lesson 2 Example 9 was offered for *he gave*. Do not infer it is unambiguously singular or plural. Preserve differences between `deout` and `deuot`, and `temis` and `aiemis`. Do not normalize without the owner's confirmation.

## Lesson 3 — native-speaker responses (received)

1. For “He gave food to that person,” the speaker confirmed: **1 use temov**.
2. She gave food to that person. — تٔمۍ دِژ أمِس شخصس کینہہ کھیٛن۔
3. He did not give food to that person. — تٔمۍ دِژ نہٕ أمِس شخصس کانٛہہ کھیٛن۔
4. She did not say anything to me. — تٔمۍ ووٚن نہٕ مےٚ کیٚنٛہہ۔

**Evidenced features:** `نہٕ` appears in both new negative expressions; in 2 vs 3 the `دِژ` construction is followed by `نہٕ` in the negative but `کینہہ` also changes to `کانٛہہ`, so this is not a one-word minimal pair. Compared with Lesson 2 “She said to me,” the negative sentence has `نہٕ` and `کیٚنٛہہ`. The owner confirmed `Temov` is used for *he gave* as prompted, but the same word was previously supplied for *they gave*; do not generalize its gender or number.

**Owner follow-up:** The native speaker subsequently supplied the standalone pronouns `سۅ` (she) and `سُہ` (he) and stated that the rest of the previous examples are correct. The Lesson 3 examples remain accepted as provided, including the `تٔمۍ` clauses. The contrast between the standalone pronouns and the forms used in completed past-tense clauses requires a grammatical paradigm before generalizing.
